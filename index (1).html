Advisory rules and real enforcement are different things

Most "AI blocking" products blur this line. We don't. Every level is labelled by how it actually works.

Asks politely

Levels 1–2. robots.txt and policy files tell well-behaved crawlers what you want. Major AI companies publish crawler names and say they honour these rules, but nothing forces a crawler to comply.

Adds friction

Level 3. A JavaScript script deters casual copying in a browser. It runs on the visitor's side, so it cannot stop a bot that requests your pages directly.

Actually blocks

Levels 4–5. Enforcement happens at your server or CDN, before content is sent. This is the only layer that can refuse a request, and it needs a platform that supports it.

How it works

  1. Choose a level. Start with the free-to-try Level 1 generator or compare all five on the pricing page.
  2. Paste your existing rules. We merge new rules into your current robots.txt and never delete what you already have.
  3. Preview, then download. You see exactly which lines are new before anything is saved. Files are generated in your browser and never uploaded.
  4. Install and verify. Step-by-step instructions for placing the file at your site root and checking it is live.

Frequently asked questions

Will this stop AI companies using my content?

Not with certainty. Compliant crawlers from major AI companies state that they follow robots.txt, so Levels 1–2 can reduce collection by those crawlers. Anything that ignores the rules can only be stopped by server- or CDN-level enforcement (Levels 4–5), and even then determined scrapers can sometimes evade detection. No product can promise 100% protection.

Is robots.txt a security feature?

No. It is a public request, not access control. Anyone can read it, and a malicious bot can ignore it. It is best treated as a clear, machine-readable statement of your preferences.

Will blocking these crawlers remove me from Google Search?

Not by default. Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended are control tokens for AI training and do not affect normal search indexing. Some AI search crawlers (such as OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot) are listed separately in the generator because blocking them can remove you from those AI search products.

Does JavaScript protection stop scrapers?

No. A script only runs in a real browser. A bot that downloads your HTML or calls your API directly never executes it. Level 3 deters casual copying and is not a substitute for server-side protection.

Can this work on GitHub Pages?

Levels 1–3 can: they produce static files and scripts. Levels 4–5 cannot run on GitHub Pages alone because it serves static files and cannot run request-filtering logic. You would put a CDN such as Cloudflare in front of your domain (a custom domain is required), and we list the exact account and permissions needed.

Do I need to be the site owner?

Yes. The generator asks you to confirm you own the domain or are authorised to manage it. Never share passwords or API keys with us; none of the current tools ask for them.

Does my robots.txt get uploaded anywhere?

No. The generator runs entirely in your browser. Your pasted file and settings never leave your device.

Limitations of crawler blocking. robots.txt and similar files are advisory. JavaScript runs on the visitor's device and cannot control what is requested directly from your server. Server-side blocking depends on identifying crawlers by signals such as user-agent strings, IP ranges and behaviour, all of which can be forged or change over time. AI Barricade reduces unwanted collection; it does not guarantee that content cannot be copied. Crawler names are maintained from public documentation and may be out of date.
